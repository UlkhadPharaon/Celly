# Hand-tracking verification screenshots

All images were captured from the real app with Playwright + ANGLE SwiftShader at 1280 × 800 (dev
server on `:5173`, same render/gesture pipeline as the build). The hand poses are deterministic
21-landmark sequences generated by
`src/core/handPoses.ts`; they are **not** webcam recordings. The same landmark data is committed as
replay fixtures in [`tests/fixtures/hand-poses/`](../../../tests/fixtures/hand-poses/) and can be
regenerated with `node scripts/write-hand-fixtures.mjs`.

`node scripts/verify-hands.mjs` produced the pose/debug/selection images and the raw numbers in
[`docs/hand-verification.json`](../../hand-verification.json). The hand is drawn as **21 joints and
21 bones**, so the meaningful checks are the drawn index tip against the pointer, the wrist against
the tracked wrist, and the joint/bone counts — not skin weights or joint angles.

## Measured summary

- Skeleton source: **skeleton** in every sample; **21 points / 21 segments** throughout.
- Wrist projection offset: **0.37 px mean / 0.43 px max** on the static poses (0.04–0.05 % of screen
  height); **0.42 px mean / 0.47 px max** across all 50 static, motion and left-hand samples.
- Drawn index-tip offset from the cursor: **0 px** in every pose and motion frame. The index-tip joint
  is placed on the camera ray through the pointer, so this is exact rather than merely close.
- Pinch-contact offset from the thumb/index midpoint: **0.07 px mean / 0.12 px max**.
- The old skin/retarget checks are gone: there is no mesh, skin weight, bone-length or joint-angle
  pipeline left to audit. The skeleton upload is ~0.1 ms in the debug sample, dominated by the two
  line buffers and one point cloud per hand.
- Selection test: **10/10 hits, 100 %, 1.6 px mean aim error**. The first target includes the
  approach from 110 px away and measured 11.5 px; the other nine landed within 1.4 px. Both are far
  inside the **52.6 px** pick radius.

The metrics are measured at the reference 1280 × 800 aspect. On a portrait phone the normalized
camera coordinates are stretched by the viewport mapping, while the skeleton deliberately keeps the
metric hand shape; the drawn index tip is still anchored to the cursor exactly, which is the number
that matters for aiming.
